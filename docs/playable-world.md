# Playable National Mall

The home route now opens a dedicated Three.js world. `/map` retains the wider Mapbox explorer and its existing HUD, tours, and discovery systems.

## Implemented

- Approximately 1.3 km of local metric world coordinates: Lincoln Memorial, Reflecting Pool, World War II Memorial, Washington Monument.
- Original procedural architectural models: a complete Lincoln colonnade, continuous climbable stair landing, chamber and simplified seated sculpture; WWII pillar ring and fountain jets; tapered obelisk, stone courses and animated flags.
- Tree-lined paths with instanced tree geometry, benches, lamps, paving, surface textures, reflective pool and distant civic silhouettes.
- Rapier capsule movement at a fixed 60 Hz: walking, running, jumping, slope/step handling, grounded collision, plus a separate free-flight mode and ground placement when returning to walking.
- Drag look, optional double-click pointer lock, keyboard controls, and coarse-pointer movement buttons with independent look-pointer tracking.
- Daylight/golden-hour lighting, nearby shadows, lower-cost quality mode, automatic resolution/shadow reduction after sustained low frame rate, distant grove culling. Landmark silhouettes stay visible along the route.
- 5 Hz React telemetry; renderer and physics state remain outside React.
- Opt-in synthesized footsteps, wind, and distance-based fountain audio; existing local narration for Lincoln and Washington.
- Four-stop route guide, explicit fast travel, minimap, nearby discoveries, help/settings and renderer failure fallback.

## Persistence

Route progress uses `dc-world-route-progress`. Only canonical Lincoln and Washington discoveries also update the existing city-map progress. Pool and WWII stops do not inflate the old map's landmark completion count. Restricted storage does not block world initialization.

## Asset origin and limits

All newly introduced 3D meshes and surface/flag textures are generated by the source in `app/lib/world/buildWorld.ts`; no third-party model downloads or new paid credentials are required. Existing narration remains in `public/audio`.

This is a stylized architectural interpretation, not surveyed geometry or a photorealistic digital twin. The modeled route is bounded; distant buildings establish context, not full-city coverage. There is no streamed external city tileset, pedestrian traffic, multiplayer, or general building interiors. The Lincoln chamber is accessible. Physics uses simplified solid shapes; flight intentionally has no building collision. Grove visibility limits rendering cost but the small route's geometry is generated up front rather than network-streamed.

## East Mall / city streaming expansion

The playable route now reaches the Capitol (approximately 3.3 km), with six stops including Smithsonian gardens. The Capitol and red-sandstone Smithsonian Castle are original modeled interpretations; their generic GIS footprints are excluded. Existing Capitol narration is reused.

`public/world/manifest.json` indexes 1,447 DC GIS building footprints in 115 tiles. `cityStreaming.ts` retains a 5 × 5 neighborhood, limits requests to four, uploads at most one tile per animation frame, aborts obsolete requests, retries failed requests, and removes departed tile geometry and collision bodies. Near facades use instanced window surrounds, sills, cornices, and original masonry textures; facade detail is hidden beyond 450 m. Walking waits at unloaded building tiles; flight remains available. Ground and the curated Mall landmarks remain resident. The maximum resident tile count is bounded, but this is not a fixed GPU-memory budget: tile densities differ.

`public/world/roads.json` contains 703 DDOT street segments; these are rendered at their source coordinates with illustrative widths and sidewalk strips. Ground remains flat; bridges, terrain, water beyond the original pool, and accessible interiors are not supplied by this expansion. Building heights are estimated from footprint area, and facades are architectural approximations, not surveyed elevations. No fabricated shop names or New York-specific fire escapes are introduced. The fetched corridor is central DC, not every ward.

Sources and reproducible baking:

The world UI now reads cream, terracotta, text, and shadow-edge values from `minecraftTheme`, used by the original dock, and restores its compact monospace control labels and tighter corners. ## Full District coverage (supersedes corridor limits above)

All 34,964 source street/path segments are included, along with 1,561 waterbody features, 252 local park features, and 456 national-park features. `scripts/build-district.py` reproduces the bake with an on-disk source cache. The older corridor-only scripts are historical and should not be used to regenerate the production world.

A persistent low-detail building layer, instanced in 2 km cells, preserves the entire city's visible mass beyond detailed tiles. It is replaced locally by footprint geometry, facades, and collision meshes. Detail remains bounded to 25 neighborhood tiles, with four requests and one tile build per animation frame. Water polygons prevent walking on open water while allowing crossings along mapped roads; the curated reflecting pool retains its original lower bed. A citywide minimap shows the current position against source streets, parks, and water.

Important distinctions: district-wide geographic coverage is implemented, but not survey-grade architecture. Distant buildings use bounding-box silhouettes, near buildings use actual footprint outlines with estimated heights and generated facades. Ground is flat, bridge decks are schematic, and most interiors are unavailable. Original bespoke Mall landmarks remain. ## Open-world movement and travel

Walking now defaults to 7 m/s, sprinting to 18 m/s; flight defaults to 120 m/s with a 500 m/s boost. The movement slider supports 0.5–3×. These are deliberately exploration speeds, not real pedestrian speeds. Flight follows camera pitch, and acceleration/deceleration respond more quickly. R or the Cruise control toggles hands-free forward movement; Escape, backward input, losing focus, or Stop cancels it. Touch controls include boost.

M / Go anywhere opens an interactive, keyboard-accessible map. Any point can be selected; the minimap also supports immediate point travel. Named travel closes the guide and preserves the user's movement mode. Walking arrivals temporarily use flight while nearby geometry loads, then search up to 120 m for clear, low ground instead of placing the player on a roof or silently refusing. Where no landing is found, the player remains in flight with an explanation. Movement cancels a pending landing.

33 tests pass, including faster-speed wall collisions, camera-relative flight normalization, map coordinate clamping, and nearest-clear-landing selection. Production build passes.

## Street fidelity and game HUD

The HUD restores a visible six-stop Mall discovery journal, earned stamps, a nearest undiscovered landmark objective with direct travel, and movement instruments with speed, heading, altitude in flight, and adjustable travel pace. It uses the existing cream/terracotta theme, with the incumbent blue flight-instrument accent. No invented XP or completion claims are added.

Nearby road surfaces now come from the official DC GIS **2021 Planimetrics** road and sidewalk polygons, including intersection shapes, islands, parking areas, sidewalks, and 13,723 crosswalk features. The crosswalk polygon extents are sourced; stripe pattern, pavement materials, and surface height offsets are illustrative. This is dated survey geometry, not a claim of current lane-by-lane accuracy. Signals, street signs, lane markings, elevation, and exact facades still need separate source-backed work.

Reproduce with `python3 scripts/build-street-surfaces.py` then `node scripts/bake-street-surfaces.mjs`. The first command caches paginated source data under `/tmp/dc-street-surfaces`; the second triangulates, clips to 1,024 m cells, normalizes winding, and writes binary geometry and attribution into `public/world/streets`. The complete asset collection is about 108 MiB, but clients retain at most nine cells, download two at a time, and upload one cell per frame. Simplified DDOT centerline roads remain as the distant/loading fallback, masked only where detailed cells are ready. Departed tiles are disposed, stale downloads are canceled, and failed requests retry.

Validation: 35 tests passed, including all baked surface bounds/winding and street streaming eviction/disposal; production build passed. Browser checks covered the discovery journal, pace controls, blue flight instruments, Georgetown crossing geometry, and desktop/mobile layouts. Browser error/warning log was empty.

## Architectural character pass

The repeated universal facade was replaced with separate low-rise residential and larger-building proportions. Ten masonry colors, finer brick courses, dark roof surfaces, modest illustrative roofline variation, narrower divided windows, cornice brackets, chimneys, paneled colored doors, shallow stoops and railings provide more street-level detail. Entrances are assigned to the footprint edge nearest sampled mapped street geometry. The Georgetown-area treatment adds shutters; other residential forms can use shallow projecting window surrounds. Large waterfront buildings receive a cooler palette and wider glazing. Distant silhouettes receive corresponding color variation.

These styles are procedural interpretations, not surveyed elevations or exact historic-district classifications. DC Office of Planning's preservation guidance on bay-front rowhouses informed the vocabulary: https://planning.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/op/publication/attachments/Door%20Guidelines.pdf . Footprints remain sourced. Facade attachments are visual detail, not newly modeled interiors; existing building collision remains in place. The detail generator batches attachments into at most six meshes per tile.

Validation: 36 tests passed, including street-facing entrance placement and facade batching; production build passed. Street-level Georgetown inspection confirmed finer brick scale and divided windows.

## Time, seasons, and small-scale detail

The sun control opens five lighting scenes (dawn, daylight, golden hour, blue hour, night) and five seasonal scenes (spring, cherry blossoms, summer, fall, winter). Lighting transitions smoothly except when reduced motion is preferred. Night includes a moon, warm window emission, glowing Mall lamps, and a bounded pair of nearby lamp lights. These are authored scenes, not a live date/weather simulation.

Tree canopies use shared, rounded lobe geometry with modeled branches. Fall varies foliage color by tree; winter removes deciduous canopies, retains an illustrative evergreen subset, and adds light snowfall. Tidal Basin cherry plantings follow mapped shoreline edges and NPS's documented regional cherry-tree distribution, but individual planting/species assignments remain illustrative. Blossom particles are limited to the basin area. Ground palettes change with the season.

A bounded group of 36 pedestrians and eight cyclists animates along authored Mall paths. It can be disabled in the atmosphere panel; reduced-motion preference freezes this animation and disables particles. These are ambient figures, not real people or traffic feeds.

Bench components now form a connected steel frame supporting separate seat/back slats and arms. A connectivity test checks that every component reaches a grounded support in both orientations. Rendering defaults to up to 2× device resolution, softer/stabilized shadows, and sharper facade texture filtering. Automatic quality reduction drops shadows before reducing image resolution.

Reference: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/cherryblossom/types-of-trees.htm and https://home.nps.gov/subjects/cherryblossom/maps.htm .

## Smithsonian museum loop

The Explorer journal has a Museums tab for Natural History, Air and Space, and American History. Each opens an original walkable 3D gallery with three Smithsonian-sourced exhibit stories, collision-safe movement, touch controls, an indoor map, and guided walking through room doorways. Entering pauses outdoor simulation and sound; leaving resumes it at the exterior arrival. Nearby exterior entry prompts and blue map markers connect the museums to DC.

Inspecting a display earns 25 XP; its question earns 50 XP once. Three completed questions earn that museum’s passport stamp. Progress is versioned in `dc-smithsonian-passport-v1` local storage, with unknown IDs discarded and duplicate rewards prevented. Narration uses the browser’s speech synthesis on request. These are three curated interpretive galleries, not full replicas of Smithsonian interiors, not all Smithsonian museums, and not live exhibition availability. Exhibit models are original simplified reconstructions. Primary source links appear on every exhibit card.
